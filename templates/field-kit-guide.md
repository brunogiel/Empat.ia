# Interview Guides

Suggested duration: 45-60 minutes. If there is a translator, complex context or a field visit, consider 60-90 minutes.

The guide is a cheat sheet, not a questionnaire. It helps you cover learning objectives without killing the conversation.

## Principles

- The interviewee is the expert.
- One person leads and another takes notes when possible.
- Open questions, not yes/no.
- Move from context and day-to-day toward depth.
- Ask for stories, examples and recent situations.
- Always ask why.
- Leave silences.
- Don't sell or validate a solution.
- Don't finish what the interviewee is saying.

### 1. Start, rapport and consent

Goal: that they understand the purpose without bias and feel comfortable.

Questions / script:

- Thanks for taking the time. We're researching how people live and solve [topic], we're not here to sell you anything.
- I'm going to ask you questions about your experience. There are no right answers.
- We want to learn from you because you're the one who lives this in practice.
- Can we record so we don't miss any details? The info will only be used for this project.

### 2. Context and day-to-day

Goal: understand routine, role, environment and responsibilities.

- Briefly tell me what you do and what your role is.
- How does this topic show up in your day-to-day?
- What happens in a normal week?
- What changes when there's urgency, a demand peak or month-end closing?

### 3. Current journey

Goal: reconstruct the real process, step by step.

- Thinking about the last time [situation] happened to you, how did it start?
- What did you do first?
- What did you do next?
- Who else was involved?
- What tools, documents, people or channels did you use?
- Where did it get stuck?
- How did it end?

### 4. Concrete experiences

Goal: avoid abstract opinions.

- Do you remember a time it went really well? What happened?
- Do you remember a time it went badly or was frustrating? What happened?
- What did you do when that failed?
- What did you learn from that situation?

### 5. Decisions, motivations and incentives

Goal: understand what drives the behavior.

- How do you decide what to do or who to call?
- What weighs most heavily in that decision?
- What risk do you want to avoid?
- What would make you change how you solve it?
- What would make you not change even if the problem still exists?

### 6. Workarounds and current alternatives

Goal: discover informal solutions and real competition.

- How did people solve it before?
- What do you use today even if it isn't ideal?
- What part have you solved with WhatsApp, Excel, paper, acquaintances or favors?
- What alternative did you try and drop?

### 7. Ideal scenario and aspirations

Goal: open up future needs without inducing a solution.

- If this worked much better, what would have to happen?
- What would an ideal experience look like?
- What couldn't be missing?
- What would be a signal that "this works"?

### 8. Closing

- Is there anything important I didn't ask?
- Is there anyone else I should talk to?
- Can we reach out again if a question comes up?

## Forbidden questions or to be reframed

| Avoid | Better ask |
|---|---|
| Would you like to use an app for this? | How do you solve this situation today? |
| Would you pay for this? | What does it cost today to solve it badly? |
| Does this seem useful to you? | At what specific moment would something like this have helped you? |
| Which feature do you want? | What part of the process creates the most friction for you? |
